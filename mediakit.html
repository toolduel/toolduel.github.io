What this channel is

A benchmark that publishes its own conflict of interest

Every episode gives the identical coding prompt to three systems — Google's free Gemini tier, a Qwen2.5-Coder 7B running locally on an RTX 3060, and Claude Code. All three open in the same browser, record for nine seconds, and get measured the same way: generation time, lines of code, distinct colours in a frame, pixel-difference motion, and JavaScript errors captured live from the console.

Claude Code also wrote the measurement harness. That is disclosed in every video, and no winner is declared in any comparison it takes part in — including episodes where it clearly produced the better output. When Google's free tier beat it on colour richness, the video said so.

What is actually for saleand what is not

At 5 subscribers, reach is not the product. The measurement is. A tool company can commission a duel, receive the recorded runs and the raw numbers, and reuse the finished video in its own marketing. The audience the channel already reaches is a bonus, not the invoice.

  • A sponsor can buy inclusion in a duel. Same prompt, same browser, same instrumentation.
  • A sponsor cannot buy the result. If the tool loses, the video says it lost.
  • Every number stays reproducible. The exact brief is published in the pinned comment of every episode; raw recordings and the harness go to anyone who asks.
  • Disclosure is on screen, not buried in a description.

A benchmark that can be bought is worth nothing to the next sponsor. Protecting that is the business model, not a courtesy.
